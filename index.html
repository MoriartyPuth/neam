How we taught a small model to find names in ខ្មែរ news

Neam (នាម, "name") finds people, organizations and places in Khmer text and points to their exact position. This page walks through how it was built: the problem, every step of the pipeline, every experiment that worked or failed, and what the results really mean.

1 · The problem

Khmer has no spaces and no capital letters

In English, "Hun Manet" stands out: capital letters, spaces around it. Khmer writes words together, has no letter case, and web pages hide invisible zero-width characters inside the text. So the first question is not "which words are names?" but "where are the words at all?"

Gloss: "On this important occasion, [Mr. ងួន ស្តេចភក្តី]PER, personal adviser to Samdech Moha Borvor Thipadei [Hun Manet]PER, Prime Minister of [Cambodia]LOC and Secretary of State of the [Ministry of Cults and Religion]ORG…" · From a held-out Fresh News article.

Little labelled data

Public Khmer NER data is scarce and mostly Wikipedia, not news. We had to label our own news articles.

Long, nested names

Organizations like ក្រសួងធម្មការ និងសាសនា contain "and", places and titles. Where does the name start and stop?

Strict scoring

We count a name as correct only if its start, end and type are all exactly right. Half a name is a miss.

What did not work

Negative results are results. These were tried, measured and dropped.

A 10× bigger multilingual model (XLM-R base)71.8 vs 76.3 F1 for the small Khmer model. A Khmer-only vocabulary beat sheer size.
Extra pretraining on Khmer news74.0 vs 76.3. Domain-adaptive pretraining did not help, even after fixing two bugs in it.
More articles, chosen without a plan31 articles scored 74.4 vs 78.0 for 21 well-chosen ones. Volume alone hurt.
A fourth batch of 10 reviewed articles (v27)84.0 / 84.5 vs 82.3 / 86.7: better on one set, worse on the other. Returns had run out.
4 · The data

Labelling: consistency beats volume

The model learns whatever the labels say, so the labels must agree with each other. We wrote a policy, then labelled news in small batches chosen where the model was least sure.

The annotation policy, in short

✓
លោក ហ៊ុន ម៉ាណែតPER short honorific stays with the name
✗
សម្ដេចមហាបវរធិបតី ceremonial titles stay outside the name
✓
រាជរដ្ឋាភិបាលកម្ពុជាORG a government with its country is one organization
✗
ជនជាតិឥណ្ឌូនេស៊ី "Indonesian nationals" describes people, not a place
✓
ប្រជាជនកម្ពុជាLOC a country after "people" stays a place
✓
ក្រុមបាល់ទាត់ខេត្តត្បូងឃ្មុំORG a sports team is one name, province included
✗
products, car models, events, treaties, medals and storms are not tagged

What the model learned from

About 1,230 training records and 3,200 labelled names. News and the curriculum are repeated three times per pass. A 64-sentence nationality curriculum taught the "ជនជាតិ" rule that 1,000 general sentences had not.

The labelling loop

1Rank

Run each of the model's members on every unlabelled article and count the names they disagree on.

2Draft

An AI assistant drafts the names for the top 10 as an ordered list, following the policy.

3Validate

A script turns each name into exact character offsets and rejects any that do not fit word boundaries.

4Review

A colour-coded review sheet goes to the human owner, who checks every label.

5Store

Only the URL, a text fingerprint and the offsets are kept. Publishers' text is never redistributed.

↻ Repeat for each batch of ten articles, then retrain and re-rank.

Technical detail: how disagreement is measured

For article a, let Sₘ(a) be the set of (start, end, type) spans predicted by member m. The disputed count is D(a) = |∪ₘ Sₘ(a) \ ∩ₘ Sₘ(a)|: names at least one member found but not all members agree on. This is query-by-committee active learning. Batches 1–3 used three members, batch 4 used seven.

5 · Results

What the released model can and cannot do

Released model v26: seven small models averaged. Scores are exact-span F1 on 10 held-out news articles the model never saw during training.

By type of name

People are almost perfect (one miss in 34). Organizations are the weak spot: the model over-predicts them and draws their boundaries wrongly, mostly on long or nested names.

How much is luck? Seed variance

The same recipe trained with different random seeds gives models up to 8.6 points apart. Averaging more of them is better and steadier.

v24 recipev26 recipe (released)

How sure are we?

Resampling the test articles 10,000 times gives a range for each score. Ten articles is a small test, so the ranges are wide, but the overall gain is unmistakable.

Still hard

មហាសន្និបាតអង្គការសហប្រជាជាតិ → finds only the inner "អង្គការសហប្រជាជាតិ" (nested names)
កីឡដ្ឋានព្រះខ័នរាជស្វាយរៀង (a stadium) → tagged as an organization, like the club
ពូទីន (Putin) → tagged as a place (foreign names)
ស្ថានទូត ("embassy", generic) → tagged as an organization
6 · Lessons

What we would tell someone starting today

1

Measure before you model

The biggest single jump (+14 F1) came from fixing how predictions were turned back into text spans, not from training.

2

Cheap structural fixes first

Reading the right subword and constrained decoding cost nothing to train and added 5–6 F1.

3

Consistency beats volume

A written policy and 64 targeted sentences fixed what 1,000 extra sentences could not. One wrong label in 284 moved a score by 2 points.

4

A small test needs two tests

Five articles could not tell models apart; a second held-out set, seven seeds and confidence intervals could.

7 · Use it

Try Neam yourself

Install and run

Runs offline on a CPU. The first call downloads the seven-model ensemble (263 MB) from Hugging Face.

pip install neam
from neam import Neam
ner = Neam.from_pretrained("MoriartyPuth/neam")
result = ner.predict("លោក ហ៊ុន ម៉ាណែត បានជួបជាមួយក្រសួងសុខាភិបាល")
for e in result["entities"]:
    print(e["label"], e["text"], e["start"], e["end"])
